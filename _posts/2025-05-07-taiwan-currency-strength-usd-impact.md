A stronger currency sounds like good news. For Taiwan right now, it's a problem measured in trillions.

The New Taiwan Dollar (TWD) recently hit its highest level against the US dollar in over three years. Normally you'd read that as confidence in the economy. But Taiwan holds close to $1 trillion in USD-denominated assets, and those are worth less every time the TWD gets stronger.

## The math

Think about this. Say the rate moves from 32 TWD per dollar to 28.

- At 32: $1 trillion = TWD 32 trillion
- At 28: $1 trillion = TWD 28 trillion

That's a TWD 4 trillion loss on paper. Nobody sold anything. The assets didn't change. Only the exchange rate did.

## Why it hurts Taiwan

1. Reserves: The central bank and the big insurers hold a lot of this in dollars. Their books take the hit in TWD.
2. Exports: Taiwan runs on exports, chips especially. A stronger TWD makes all of it more expensive for foreign buyers.
3. Panic: As dollar assets lose value in TWD terms, holders rush to cut their exposure. That selling pushes the TWD up further, which makes the next round of losses bigger.

## Why it matters outside Taiwan

If Taiwan, or another big holder like Japan, starts unloading dollar assets, you get:

- more USD selling,
- a weaker dollar,
- higher US bond yields, because there are fewer foreign buyers for Treasuries.

## Any upside?

A few. Imports get cheaper (oil, raw materials, food), which helps keep inflation down. People travelling or studying abroad get more for their money.

I don't think that makes up for the hit to reserves and exports, though.

Taiwan's problem here isn't failure. It's success arriving too fast.
